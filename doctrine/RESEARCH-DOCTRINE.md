# The CoinPicks Research Doctrine

**Read this at the START of every research session.** This is the running record of how the method calls for altcoin research done. Updated continuously as the author corrects / refines the workflow.

If you're a new chat picking this up: this file overrides any defaults. Match the voice and discipline below, no exceptions.

---

## MANDATORY SELF-AUDIT before any research verdict (added 2026-07-15) — ENFORCEMENT IS YOURS, NOT THE READER'S

**This runs automatically, every time, and I self-flag BEFORE the author has to.** The TIG incident (2026-07-15, see ERROR-LOG) happened because I mis-framed the business model and defaulted to reflexive skepticism. The deeper mistake was leaving it to the author to catch. Never again. Do NOT phrase safeguards as "call it out and I'll fix it" — that offloads the job to him. I catch it myself.

Before delivering ANY verdict on a project's value / demand / quality, stop and run this gate:

1. **Define the business archetype first.** One sentence: "This is a [Arm/Dolby IP-licensing? SaaS? marketplace? toll road? royalty?] that makes money by [mechanism]; the buyer's real alternative today is [X]; switching gains/costs are [Y]." If I cannot write it, I am not ready to judge it. Research the model first.
2. **Bearish-tell trip-wire.** Am I uniformly negative on a project with a REAL team + a REAL product, and do my objections reduce to "not independently verified / a free alternative exists / they build it in-house"? If yes, I have very likely mis-framed the model. STOP, say so in my own response ("I may have mis-framed this, rebuilding the frame"), rebuild it, and only then deliver. This is the exact signature of the TIG error.
3. **Steelman before refuting.** the author's handed-over thesis/notes are the PRIMARY hypothesis to strengthen, not a footnote to override with a generic swarm. Point any adversarial/skeptic pass at the real model, not a strawman.
4. **"Free exists" / "unverified" discipline.** A free alternative only refutes a licensing model if the free path is actually usable for THAT buyer. "Unverified" gets labeled, never scored as a negative; use reputational/structural priors instead.
5. **Three separate questions.** Is it real/good (priors) / will the market pay (structure) / does the token capture it (mechanics) — a weakness in one is NOT a weakness in the others.

If any trip-wire fires, flag it myself in the response and rebuild the frame. Bearish and hedged is NOT the same as honest; honest means calibrated, which can be confidently positive.

## Voice rules (non-negotiable)

- **No financial advice.** No "buy this", "moon", "bullish", "this could 10x." Just state what we see.
- **No shilling adjectives.** Strip *exciting / groundbreaking / revolutionary / game-changing / next-level* before writing.
- **Plain language.** Members are smart but not always crypto-native. Explain like you'd explain to a sharp friend over coffee.
- the author's exact framing: *"We're not trying to sell them on buying this. Literally, just telling them what we see."*

---

## Facts discipline

- **Don't invent numbers.** If a metric can't be sourced, omit it. Trust gone if you fake one.
- **Every claim needs a Ctrl+F-able quote** in the citation: `[url] "exact quote text"`. The reader must be able to verify on the page.
- **Team credibility = past employers ONLY.** Never use the current project's metrics to score the founder. Founder's prior exit, prior employer revenue, prior research citations — never "they raised $X for the current project" as a team credibility signal.
- **When first-party numbers don't exist, estimate from sourced proxies and label it.** Don't be paralyzed by lack of perfect data. the author on RAIN: *"you can actually say or guess how much money Polymarket makes in profit?"* — yes, estimate, but flag it as an estimate with the proxy you used.

## Structure (mandatory format)

- **"What $TICKER does" callout in every Overview.** One sentence on actual utility (gas token, staking yield, fee burn, governance, etc.).
- **Yellow-highlight every H1** with `<span color="yellow_bg">...</span>`.
- **One sentence per team member** + 1-3 sourced links with quoted text from the source.
- **5-section template:** Overview → Exclusivity Factor → Narrative Scoring → Team → (optional Extra).
- **Analogy callout** in every Overview (postcard vs sealed envelope, Airbnb for GPUs, etc.).

---

## Angle / thesis discipline (THE BIG ONE)

- **Lead with the angle.** If there's an asymmetric thesis, it goes FIRST in the Overview. Don't bury it in paragraph 3.
- **Don't undercut the angle with pedantic skepticism.** When you write "but actually…" three times in the Overview, the thesis dies on the page.
- the author on POD: *"you took out the angle. The whole angle, bro, is that this is a play on the virality of Venice AI."*
- Risks belong in the **risk section**. Not woven through every paragraph as hedges.
- The format: ENGAGE the upside thesis in the body. Put the bear case in a labeled risk block at the end.

## What a STRONG entry contains (lean into these)

These are the things that make a research entry land. They are not stylistic preferences;
each one is a form of evidence.


- **Sharp angles with comparable math** — *"40× valuation gap"*, *"1/40th of Venice's mcap"*, *"trades at $15M vs comp at $600M"*
- **Specific founders with verified prior exits** — Paillier IACR Fellowship, Hindi's $37.5M Sonos sale, founder of [previous unicorn]
- **Tier-1 VCs + L1 founders** — Multicoin, Pantera, Coinbase Ventures, Yakovenko, Gavin Wood, Benet
- **Concrete first-party adoption with numbers** — JPMorgan Kinexys ($121M live), T-REX RWAs ($100B), Venice using Dolphin-Mistral as default
- **Plain-language analogies** — postcard vs sealed envelope, Airbnb for GPUs, banks moving money but on a different rail
- **"Hair-on-Fire" framings** — concrete reasons this NEEDS to exist NOW

## The failure modes that kill an entry (avoid these)

Each of these has ruined a real entry. They are listed in roughly the order they do damage.


- **Burying the lede / missing the angle.** *"the whole angle, bro, is..."* = he's mad.
- **Pedantic skepticism.** "but actually..." every paragraph kills the entry.
- **Made-up numbers.** Caught once, trust gone.
- **Doing more than asked.** Adding diligence steps he didn't authorize (Goplus, top-holders, comp screens he didn't request).
- **Telling him the system can do things it can't.** Don't claim capability without verifying. *"Liar."*
- **Duplicates / forgetting context.** Always check what already exists.
- **Long-winded responses.** the method calls for tight architectural takes. Not lectures.
- **Acknowledging summary / recapping.** When picking up mid-task, just continue.

## Style rules — self-contained entries (CRITICAL)

**Every Notion entry must be readable cold by someone with zero context.** the author's feedback (2026-06-01): *"Each entry in this database needs to be self-contained and readable on its own. Right now, there's not enough detail. I still don't know what USD.AI does."*

### Apply these rules to every entry going forward AND retrofit existing ones:

1. **Explain what the project actually DOES** — step by step, in plain language. Don't assume the reader knows the category. If it's a lending protocol: who lends to whom, what's the collateral, how is the rate set, what happens on default.

2. **Specific numbers always.** Vague phrases like *"clean combination of two structural shifts"* or *"meaningful revenue"* fail. Replace with: *"$358M+ originated, $13M Series A led by Framework, 60% of protocol fees route to token buybacks."* Numbers > adjectives.

3. **Token value accrual must be spelled out concretely.** "Governance token" alone isn't enough. Say: WHAT percentage of revenue routes to buybacks? WHEN does the fee switch flip? IS there a burn? IS there a stake-to-earn yield? IF holders get nothing today, say that explicitly.

4. **Name the closest competitor and how this differs.** If reader can't tell what makes this different from 5 other projects in the same category, the entry isn't done.

5. **YZi Labs → always append "(formerly Binance Labs)"** at least the first time it appears in any entry. Many readers won't know the rebrand.

6. **Verify every past-employer claim before writing.** WebSearch the founder's name + the claimed company. If you can't find a public source (LinkedIn, press release, Crunchbase), DO NOT WRITE THE CLAIM. Past Phase 2 agents have hallucinated past-employer details — every credential needs a sourced URL with Ctrl+F-able quote.

### Anti-patterns the author called out
- *"the cleanest combination of two structural shifts on the list at once"* → vague comparative, reader doesn't know what's being combined
- *"thesis is solid"* → meaningless without specifics
- *"strong VC stack"* → which firms, how much, when
- *"established product"* → how established, what numbers prove it

## Swarms vs single agents

When the author says "use a swarm" or "use a team of agents," he means **8-15 parallel agents minimum**, not 2-3. Two agents is not a swarm. Fan out properly:

- 50-coin research → 10 agents × 5 coins each, or 10 agents × different categories/chains
- Bulk DB upgrades → 1 agent per entry (15 agents for 15 entries)
- Multi-angle research on a single project → 6-8 agents each with a different lens
- Cross-source validation → 1 agent per data source, run in parallel

When in doubt about whether to fan out wider, default to MORE agents not fewer. Cost is tokens not dollars.

## Chart liveness and dead-project filters (added 2026-06-18)

the author is tired of looking at dead charts. Apply these as hard pre-filters before proposing OR adding a coin:

- **The dead-project rule (the author's exact heuristic):** if a coin was EVER above ~$50M market cap but now does LESS than $1M in 24-hour trading volume, assume the project is dead and skip it. Use this as the proxy since the system cannot see the chart visually. Always pull 24h volume + peak/ATH mcap and run this test.
- **Flat / bled-out charts = skip.** Long slow decline to near-zero with no recovery = dead. Example he flagged: Spectra (SPECTRA) at ~$1.3M mcap, ~$2.7K 24h vol, once ~$0.25 = dead.
- **Rollups are dead to the author.** Do NOT propose generic rollups / modular-execution / L2 plays "unless there's something crazy about it." Exceptions he made on 2026-06-18: Aztec (privacy + Zac Williamson / PLONK pedigree), Monad and MegaETH (high-performance L1 / real-time L2 with Jump / Stanford / MIT founders and huge raises). - **Pivoted / abandoned projects = skip.** If the team keeps posting about a DIFFERENT project, treat the named token as abandoned. Example: Spectral (SPEC) keeps posting about "The Grid" → skipped.
- **Whole-category dead charts.** On 2026-06-18 he rejected most of the DeFi-lane candidates (Drift, Resolv, Gearbox, Term, Spectra, Fluid) for dead charts, keeping only Kamino and Maple (SYRUP). Also rejected Aleo (ALEO) and Almanak and MANTRA (OM, "switched their coins").

## Anonymous teams: weigh them down, never exclude them (updated 2026-08-29)

An anonymous team is **not** a disqualification. Plenty of good projects run anonymous, and
crypto has a long history of pseudonymous builders who delivered.

What it is instead is **evidence you cannot get**. The Team system rests on verifiable past
work: prior employers, shipped products, real outcomes with numbers attached. When nobody is
named, none of that can be checked, so that pillar carries far less weight in your judgment
and the entry must say so out loud rather than quietly scoring around it.

So:
- **Do not filter a coin out for being anonymous.** Research it.
- **Do state plainly** that the team could not be verified, and what that costs the thesis.
- **Lean harder on what CAN be checked** when the people cannot: the product working, real
  usage, liquidity that actually trades, on-chain behaviour, funding that is verifiable.
- **Be more careful with size and time horizon.** An unverifiable team is a real risk, just
  not an automatic no.
